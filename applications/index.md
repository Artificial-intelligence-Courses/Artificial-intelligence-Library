# Applications

## Introduction

The value of artificial intelligence is realized when models leave the lab and solve real problems. Across industries, AI now powers search and recommendations, automates document and image understanding, supports decisions, and accelerates scientific discovery. This section surveys where AI is applied and what it takes to make those applications dependable.

Applying AI well is as much about problem framing, data, and integration as it is about the model itself. The strongest results come from matching a capable model to a clearly defined need and embedding it thoughtfully into an existing workflow.

## Core Capabilities

A handful of capabilities recur across applications. **Natural language processing** handles text—translation, summarization, search, and conversation. **Computer vision** interprets images and video for tasks from medical imaging to quality inspection. **Recommendation and ranking** personalize content and products. **Forecasting and anomaly detection** support planning and risk management. Most products combine several of these with conventional software and human judgment.

## Across Domains

In **healthcare**, AI assists with imaging, triage, and drug discovery, under strong requirements for safety and oversight. In **finance**, it supports fraud detection, risk modeling, and operations. In **science and engineering**, it accelerates simulation, design, and the analysis of large datasets. In **creative and knowledge work**, generative tools assist with writing, design, and coding. Each domain brings its own constraints—regulation, latency, interpretability, and the cost of errors—that shape how a system must be built.

## From Model to Product (MLOps)

Turning a model into a reliable product is an engineering discipline of its own, often called **MLOps**. It covers data pipelines, reproducible training, deployment, and the monitoring needed to catch drift and degradation over time. Production systems must address latency and cost, versioning, testing, and a path to retrain and roll back safely. Neglecting this lifecycle is a common reason promising prototypes fail to deliver lasting value.

## Measuring Impact

A successful application is judged by outcomes, not benchmark scores. Defining the right metrics, running real-world evaluations, and weighing benefits against risks—including the safety and fairness concerns covered elsewhere in the library—determine whether an AI system genuinely helps the people it serves.
