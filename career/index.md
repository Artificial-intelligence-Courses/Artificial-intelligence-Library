# Career

A career in artificial intelligence offers many entry points and room to grow as the field matures. Demand spans research, engineering, data, and product, and people arrive from backgrounds as varied as software, statistics, science, and design. Whether you are starting out or specializing further, AI rewards a mix of technical depth, practical judgment, and a habit of continuous learning.

### Roles

The field includes several distinct roles. **Machine learning engineers** build and ship models as part of production systems. **Data scientists** frame problems, analyze data, and turn findings into decisions. **Research scientists and engineers** advance methods and push capabilities forward. **MLOps and platform engineers** create the infrastructure that makes models reliable at scale. Adjacent roles in product, design, and policy shape how AI is used responsibly. Titles vary between organizations, and many positions blend these responsibilities.

### Skills

Most roles share a common core: programming, a working command of the mathematics behind learning, and fluency with data. Beyond the fundamentals, employers value the ability to evaluate models honestly, reason about trade-offs, and communicate results to non-specialists. Familiarity with the safety and fairness concerns covered elsewhere in this library is increasingly expected rather than optional. A portfolio of real projects often demonstrates capability more convincingly than credentials alone.

### Growth

Progression in AI is a continuous journey rather than a fixed ladder. The tools and best practices change quickly, so staying effective means following research, contributing to projects and communities, and learning from production experience. Building a professional network opens doors to mentorship and new opportunities, while specializing in a domain or technique helps define a long-term path. Adaptability and curiosity remain the most durable assets in a fast-moving field.
